\paragraph{D1 finite-control methods card.}
The cart roots are $(p,v)=(-.30,-1.85)$ and $(-.30,3.85)$; the pendulum roots are
$(\theta,\omega)=(-.04,-3.00)$ and $(-.04,3.25)$.  In each domain they have algebraically reachable
safe predecessors, equal current rendered frames, and distinct preceding frames.  From each root the
oracle enumerates every sequence in $\{-1,0,+1\}^3$ with zero process noise under the shipped
dynamics.  State, current-frame, and two-frame codes use exact stored-tuple equality.  Viability,
common-action membership, and the global-policy Boolean use exact stored-float signs;

\subsection{Frozen FCSRL feasibility-loss adaptation}
\label{app:fcsrl-baseline}

\begingroup\footnotesize
This prospective same-backbone offline arm is an adaptation, not a reproduction of FCSRL's online
TD3--Lagrangian agent.  It fixes data, encoder and transition architectures, reconstruction/
transition/decoder/KL losses, latent width, split, optimizer budget, and checkpoint rule.  Let
$s(y)=\operatorname{sign}(y)\log(1+|y|)$.  A head $p_{\theta,j}(z)$ uses 63 equally spaced atoms
$b_j\in[-2,4]$ in $s$-space and mean
$F_\theta(z)=s^{-1}(\sum_j b_jp_{\theta,j}(z))$.

For a 10-transition behavior-policy window, compute stop-gradient targets backward with
\[
 Y_9=\bar F_\theta(H_{10}),\qquad
 Y_t=\max\!\left\{\mathbf 1\{h(x_t)<0\},
 .9(1-d_t)\big[(1-u_t)Y_{t+1}+u_t\bar F_\theta(H_{t+1})\big]\right\},
 \quad t=8{:}0,
\]
where $d_t$ is true termination, $u_t$ is time-limit truncation, and
$\bar F_\theta(H)=F_\theta(\bar g(H))$.  The target head is stop-gradient but not EMA; after each
optimizer step, $\bar\theta_g\leftarrow.99\bar\theta_g+.01\theta_g$.  The first $K=4$ unrolled
predictions minimize cross-entropy against the piecewise-linear projection of $s(Y_t)$ onto the two
adjacent atoms.  The ending transition is retained and positions strictly after the first
termination or truncation are masked.  To match the base-example and optimizer-step exposure of the
other arms, the implementation starts one sequence at each original transition; near the horizon it
repeats the terminal history and a fixed padding action only after the retained truncation, where the
mask is zero.

This implementation remains a comparator for the legacy AE/$\beta$-VAE path.  In the foundation
study, only its categorical feasibility target is ported to each same pretrained backbone, with the
same internal-adapter placement, data, parameter budget, and fresh-head evaluation as the proposed
repair; the port is still an adaptation rather than an official reproduction
\citep{cen2024feasibility}.  No FCSRL target-latent cosine loss, actor, critic, or Lagrangian update
is transplanted.  It therefore estimates behavior-policy future violation, not the robust
counterfactual profile; the pinned legacy crosswalk is
\texttt{docs/fcsrl\_baseline\_adaptation.md}.
\endgroup

\subsection{Prospective foundation-study reporting card}

\paragraph{Executable implementation boundary.}
The dependency-light implementation under \texttt{latent\_safety.foundation} now fixes a common
visual/feature contract, a viable-first buffered neighborhood audit, a dependency-free reference
version of the hard set-wise objective, and a lazy PyTorch optimization path.  The latter includes
an identity-initialized residual bottleneck intervention for internal token features, a physically
separate action-conditioned profile head, boundary/tail profile supervision, feature and pairwise-
geometry retention, explicit neighborhood-coverage diagnostics, trainable-parameter and gradient-
route guards, and whitelist-based removal of the training head and all undeclared side modules;
dependency-free paths have executable regressions and the PyTorch paths have optional test coverage.
Checkpoint-specific wrappers must still prove that the intervention is inserted before the audited
bottleneck and must provide locally verified artifact hashes.  No Cosmos, V-JEPA, DINOv3,
ManiSkill3, or Habitat checkpoint has yet been loaded or evaluated by this code, so this executable
core changes implementation status but supplies no empirical evidence for the hypotheses.

Every audited checkpoint must record its exact version and weight hash; license; preprocessing;
input resolution, frames per second, and causal history span; selected layer and token field;
pooling or quantization; native and matched-rate dimensions; frozen and trainable parameter counts;
optimizer steps; peak memory; and wall-clock compute.  The simulator manifest records the saved
physical state, observation hash, action/skill definition, continuation rule, horizon, safety
margin, all counterfactual branches, and oracle version for every example.  Split manifests group
correlated trajectories before assigning train, calibration, validation, and test.

The evaluation manifest separately pins native-interface and standardized-interface audits,
normalization statistics, radius curves, matched-mass or fixed-$k$ neighborhoods, physical groups,
coverage, feature-rank and distance diagnostics, profile-head removal, fresh-readout training, and
the held-fixed downstream controller.  Model and radius selection use validation data only.  A
pretrained checkpoint comparison is reported as observational; only paired within-family adaptation
supports a repair-effect claim, and even that claim is conditional on the registered simulator and
action library.
