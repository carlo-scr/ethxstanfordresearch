\begin{abstract}
Learned world models compress high-dimensional observations into latent states on which planners,
safety filters, and certificates act.  Existing guarantees are typically conditional on the latent
state preserving the distinctions needed by the safety specification and controlled dynamics.  We
study when this information is lost.  Our framework separates two representation-level
obstructions: safe states that share a code with unsafe states, and individually viable states that
share a code but admit no common safe action.  We give an endpoint-correct characterization of the
best completeness threshold attainable by any sound latent safe set and show that deterministic
post-processing cannot beat observation-level ambiguity.  For safe actions, we characterize when
deterministic and randomized memoryless decisions are impossible, give a common-action
least-intervention construction, bound pointwise expected-loss randomization for finite actions,
show that randomization gives no benefit under strong fiber-wise loss, and construct smooth
encoders whose average reconstruction and prediction losses vanish while worst-case safety
ambiguity persists.  We pair these results with finite-data audits, controlled
partial-observability fixtures, and a prospectively frozen world-model study at matched utility.
Large-scale empirical claims are
intentionally withheld until that study is complete.
\end{abstract}
