\begin{abstract}
Pretrained visual representations increasingly serve as the state interface for planning, control,
and safety filters.  Their native objectives preserve pixels, semantic structure, or predictable
features, but none requires histories mapped to one code to retain a common safe action.  We call
this requirement \emph{safe-action sufficiency}.  We characterize exact static and common-action
obstructions, prove that deterministic post-processing cannot repair exact aliases, and give a
smooth construction in which average reconstruction and prediction losses can be driven
arbitrarily close to zero while worst-case safety ambiguity remains fixed.  We then define a
traceable finite-radius common-action audit and propose \emph{Common-Action Geometry Repair}: update
pre-bottleneck encoder modules using an
action-profile loss, a set-wise neighborhood objective, and a family-specific retention loss.
Unlike a safety probe, the intervention will be evaluated after removing its training head and
fitting a fresh downstream readout.  The prospective study deliberately triangulates reconstructive,
predictive, and semantic pretraining through Cosmos Tokenizer, V-JEPA~2, and DINOv3.  The protocol
is designed to match the causal information span and an explicitly quantized capacity
proxy within each comparison, and will use simulator-branched action profiles in manipulation and
embodied navigation; driving is an optional
external-validity study rather than the paper's identity.  Exact cart and pendulum aliases presently
validate only the obstruction and observation-floor controls.  No pretrained-encoder repair or
closed-loop foundation-model result is claimed before the prospectively specified study is executed.
\end{abstract}
