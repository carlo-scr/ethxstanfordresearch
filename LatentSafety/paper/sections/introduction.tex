\section{Introduction}

Pretrained visual representations make physical decision-making from pixels tractable, but their
compression and invariances are not neutral.  Cosmos Tokenizer is optimized as a reconstructive
image/video codec \citep{nvidia2025cosmos,nvidia2025cosmostokenizer}; V-JEPA~2 learns predictable
spatiotemporal features and its action-conditioned variant supports planning
\citep{assran2025vjepa2,murlabadia2026vjepa21}; DINOv3 emphasizes semantic and dense spatial
features \citep{simeoni2025dinov3}.  These objectives preserve different notions of utility, yet
none directly requires the representation to preserve \emph{which action is safe}.  A factor can
be cheap for pixels, semantics, or average prediction and still determine whether a robot should
move left, right, stop, or release an object.

This matters upstream of the controller.  Recent work designs barrier functions, reachability
values, or robust controllers directly in learned latent spaces
\citep{lutkus2025latent,anand2025safety,seo2025uncertainty,nath2026pixels}.  \citet{lutkus2025latent}
define a latent safe set containing only codes with no unsafe preimage.  Once an upstream code has
identified two physically different histories, however, no deterministic head can recover the
lost distinction.  The resulting question is not only whether a code separates safe from unsafe
states, but how much useful safe region and control authority it has made unavailable.

A static safe/unsafe collision is only the first obstruction.  Encoding the known scalar safety
margin itself separates the labels, yet states with the same margin can have different dynamics and
disjoint safe actions.  Under partial observability, a single image may not even identify velocity
or an occluded hazard.  We first ask whether one representation-measurable decision can satisfy a
declared support-wise condition.  Recursive representation-policy viability, Markov latent
dynamics, and persistent closed-loop safety require additional theory and are not established in
the main manuscript.

The scientific unit of study is therefore not autonomous driving or any one tokenizer.  It is the
mapping from a causal visual history to the feasible-action correspondence.  We compare three
families as a deliberately controlled objective triangulation---reconstructive compression,
temporal prediction, and semantic invariance---rather than attributing causal effects to checkpoints
that also differ in architecture, scale, and pretraining data.  Manipulation and embodied
navigation provide the primary, visually distinct control domains; driving remains a possible
third stress test.

The present draft contributes: (i) a specification-relative definition of \emph{safe-action
sufficiency} and a traceable neighborhood defect that distinguishes physical infeasibility from
representation-induced conflict; (ii) supporting exact results for static separation,
post-processing, stochastic codes, deterministic and randomized actions, and a smooth
average-loss separation; (iii) \emph{Common-Action Geometry Repair}, a pre-bottleneck encoder
intervention that combines action-profile supervision with a set-wise common-action objective and
family-specific utility retention; and (iv) a prospectively specified cross-family evaluation
protocol that matches causal information and an operational capacity proxy, controls readout and
downstream selection, and compares Cosmos Tokenizer,
V-JEPA~2, and DINOv3.  Exact pixel cart and pendulum controls currently validate only the finite
obstruction and observation floor.  The foundation-encoder audit, repair, and downstream claims
remain prospective, and every status is governed by the claim registry.
