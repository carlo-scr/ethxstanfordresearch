\section{Introduction}

Latent dynamics make planning from pixels tractable, but compression is not neutral: a learned code
may discard a factor that is cheap for reconstruction yet decisive for safety.  Recent work designs
barrier functions, reachability values, or robust controllers directly in learned latent spaces
\citep{lutkus2025latent,anand2025safety,seo2025uncertainty,nath2026pixels}.  The strongest transfer
results correctly define a latent safe set containing only codes with no unsafe preimage
\citep{lutkus2025latent}.  This exposes an upstream question: how much useful safe region and
control authority did the representation already make unavailable?

A static safe/unsafe collision is only the first obstruction.  Encoding the known scalar safety
margin itself separates the labels, yet states with the same margin can have different dynamics and
disjoint safe actions.  Under partial observability, a single image may not even identify velocity
or an occluded hazard.  We therefore treat the representation as an abstraction of state histories
or beliefs and ask whether it is sufficient for persistent safe decisions.

Our current contributions are: (i) an exact static soundness--completeness equivalence with open,
closed, attained, and non-attained endpoint cases; (ii) a deterministic data-processing theorem
that separates observation ambiguity from encoder-induced ambiguity; (iii) common-safe-action
impossibility results for deterministic and carefully qualified randomized decisions, together
with distinct strong and pointwise expected-loss finite-action slacks; (iv) a smooth separation
showing that vanishing average reconstruction and prediction losses need not control worst-case
safety ambiguity; and (v) an executable audit
and study protocol for testing safety-sufficient objectives against strong safety-aware baselines
at matched utility.  The first
four are foundations and separation results rather than end-to-end control guarantees.  All proof and
empirical states remain governed by the repository claim registry and independent-review rule.
