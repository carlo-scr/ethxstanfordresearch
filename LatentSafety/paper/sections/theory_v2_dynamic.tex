\section{Candidate: dynamic margin regret and representation-policy viability}
\label{sec:theory-v2-candidate}

\paragraph{Candidate status.}
This section is proof-drafted but is not yet a submission claim.  Approximate state abstractions,
state--action abstractions, and approximate information states already give policy-loss bounds;
feedback refinement and belief-support shielding already establish robust controller-refinement
and partial-observation safety structure
\citep{abel2016approximate,abel2020value,subramanian2022ais,dave2024ais,
dave2023worstcase,reissig2017feedback,sheng2024safe,girard2012safety}.
This package is an analytical instrument, not a theory-contribution claim.  It separates robust
optimal-margin regret, sign-specific first-action feasibility, and exact recursively evaluated
representation-policy viability.
In particular, \citet{dave2024ais} already analyze finite-horizon maximum instantaneous cost under
partial observation using current-cost and Hausdorff successor errors and obtain a factor-two
approximate-policy bound.  After changing sign from cost to margin, the model-local condition and
factor two below are specializations rather than standalone novelty.

\subsection{Finite support-robust history game}

Let $\mathsf A$ be a finite nonempty action set, let $T\geq0$, and let $\mathsf H_s$ be the finite
nonempty set of histories with $s\in\{0,\ldots,T\}$ controls remaining.  A history may be a
full observation/action history, belief, or belief support.  If $I(\eta)$ is its nonempty set of
compatible physical states, define the support-robust current margin
\[
  G_s(\eta)=\inf_{x\in I(\eta)}h(x).
\]
Thus $G_s(\eta)\geq0$ means that every compatible state is safe.  For $s\geq1$, let
$N_s(\eta,a)\subseteq\mathsf H_{s-1}$ be the finite nonempty set of all admissible successor
histories, including all declared disturbances and observations.  Define
\begin{align}
 V_0(\eta)&=G_0(\eta),\nonumber\\
 Q_s(\eta,a)
 &=\min\left\{G_s(\eta),\min_{\eta'\in N_s(\eta,a)}V_{s-1}(\eta')\right\},
 \label{eq:robust-history-q}\\
 V_s(\eta)&=\max_{a\in\mathsf A}Q_s(\eta,a).\nonumber
\end{align}
The action maxima and finite-successor minima are attained; the physical-state infimum already
absorbed into $G_s$ need not be.  Induction shows that $V_s(\eta)\geq0$ if and only if a deterministic
history-feedback policy keeps the physical margin nonnegative on every compatible branch for the
remaining horizon.  This is support-wise safety, not an expected cost or chance constraint.
The maps $R_s$ below are history encoders; no recursive latent update or standalone latent world
model is assumed.

Let $R_s:\mathsf H_s\to\mathcal Z_s$, with fiber
$F_{s,z}=\{\eta:R_s(\eta)=z\}$.  Define
\begin{align}
 r_s(z,a)&=\max_{\eta\in F_{s,z}}\bigl[V_s(\eta)-Q_s(\eta,a)\bigr],\nonumber\\
 \rho_s(z;R_s)&=\min_{a\in\mathsf A}r_s(z,a),\qquad
 \rho_s(R_s)=\max_{z\in R_s(\mathsf H_s)}\rho_s(z;R_s).
 \label{eq:dynamic-optimal-margin-regret}
\end{align}
We call $\rho_s$ the \emph{robust optimal-margin regret}.  It is exactly the smallest
worst-history value loss when the first action depends on the code but full-history optimal control
resumes afterward; it is not an exact safety defect.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_s(z;R_s)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bigcap_{\eta\in F_{s,z}}\arg\max_{a\in\mathsf A}Q_s(\eta,a)\neq\varnothing.
 \label{eq:zero-regret-characterization}
\end{equation}
This value-preserving condition is stronger than sign-only safety.  Let
$F_{s,z}^+=\{\eta\in F_{s,z}:V_s(\eta)\geq0\}$ and define the first-action obstruction
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_s(z;R_s)=\min_{a\in\mathsf A}\max_{\eta\in F_{s,z}^+}[-Q_s(\eta,a)]_+,
 \label{eq:first-action-obstruction}
\end{equation}
with maximum zero on an empty viable set.  Then $\kappa_s(z;R_s)=0$ exactly when one action has
$Q_s(\eta,a)\geq0$ for every viable fiber member.  This does not make the $\rho$ selector safe:
for the Q table
$\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}0&2\\0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$, the first action is common safe and
$\kappa_s=0$, whereas the unique minimax-regret action is the second action, with $\rho_s=1$, and
is unsafe in the second history.

\begin{proposition}[Optimal-margin-regret data processing]
\label{prop:dynamic-data-processing}
If $R_s^c=T_s\circ R_s^f$ is a deterministic coarsening on the same controlled history layer, then
$\rho_s(R_s^f)\leq\rho_s(R_s^c)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every fine fiber is contained in one coarse fiber.  For each action, the maximum regret over the
fine fiber is no larger than that over the coarse fiber.  Minimization over actions and then the
maximum over fibers preserve the inequality.
\end{proof}
No postprocessing premise is needed at terminal layer $s=0$, where no regret is defined.

\subsection{Arbitrary-code-policy composition and exact viability}

Let $\mu_s:\mathcal Z_s\to\mathsf A$ be any deterministic time-indexed code policy and set
\[
 \pi_s^\mu(\eta)=\mu_s(R_s(\eta)).
\]
Its recursively evaluated robust value is
\begin{align*}
 J_0^\mu(\eta)&=G_0(\eta),\\
 J_s^\mu(\eta)&=\min\left\{G_s(\eta),
 \min_{\eta'\in N_s(\eta,\pi_s^\mu(\eta))}J_{s-1}^\mu(\eta')\right\}.
\end{align*}
Define $\epsilon_s^\mu(z)=r_s(z,\mu_s(z))$,
$\epsilon_s^\mu=\max_z\epsilon_s^\mu(z)$, and the path-dependent buffer
\begin{align}
 B_0^\mu(\eta)&=0,\nonumber\\
 B_s^\mu(\eta)&=\epsilon_s^\mu(R_s(\eta))
 +\max_{\eta'\in N_s(\eta,\pi_s^\mu(\eta))}B_{s-1}^\mu(\eta').
 \label{eq:path-buffer}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrary-code-policy margin loss]
\label{thm:dynamic-representation-loss}
For every $s$ and $\eta\in\mathsf H_s$,
\begin{equation}
 0\leq V_s(\eta)-J_s^\mu(\eta)
 \leq B_s^\mu(\eta)
 \leq\sum_{j=1}^s\epsilon_j^\mu.
 \label{eq:dynamic-loss-bound}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the closed endpoint implication
\[
 V_s(\eta)\geq B_s^\mu(\eta)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad J_s^\mu(\eta)\geq0
\]
holds.  The looser condition $V_s(\eta)\geq\sum_{j=1}^s\epsilon_j^\mu$ also guarantees
support-robust safety.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Optimality gives $J_s^\mu\leq V_s$.  The remaining claim is exact for $s=0$.  Assume it holds at
$s-1$, set $a=\pi_s^\mu(\eta)$, and let
$M=\max_{\eta'\in N_s(\eta,a)}B_{s-1}^\mu(\eta')$.  The induction hypothesis gives
\[
 \min_{\eta'}J_{s-1}^\mu(\eta')
 \geq\min_{\eta'}V_{s-1}(\eta')-M.
\]
Since $\min\{g,b-M\}\geq\min\{g,b\}-M$ for $M\geq0$,
$J_s^\mu(\eta)\geq Q_s(\eta,a)-M$.  By definition,
$Q_s(\eta,a)\geq V_s(\eta)-\epsilon_s^\mu(R_s(\eta))$.  Combining the inequalities gives the
local bound in \eqref{eq:dynamic-loss-bound}; the sum follows because each local term is at most
$\epsilon_j^\mu$.  Rearrangement proves the endpoint statement.
\end{proof}

For the tie-broken minimax margin selector
$a_s^\rho(z)\in\arg\min_a r_s(z,a)$, one has
$\epsilon_s^\mu=\rho_s(R_s)$.  The resulting buffer controls margin loss but does not maximize
retained viable volume, as the counterexample above shows.

Let
$\kappa_{1:T}^{\max}(R)=\max_{1\leq j\leq T}\max_{z\in R_j(\mathsf H_j)}
\kappa_j(z;R_j)$.
When $T=0$, this and every subsequent maximum over controlled layers is defined as zero.
\begin{proposition}[Exact global sign preservation]
\label{prop:global-sign-preservation}
$\kappa_{1:T}^{\max}(R)=0$ if and only if one time-indexed representation policy $\mu$ satisfies
$V_j(\eta)\geq0\Rightarrow J_j^\mu(\eta)\geq0$ for every layer $j$ and history $\eta$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If all obstructions vanish, select a common-safe action in each fiber.  Inductively, its
nonnegative Q value makes the current margin safe and sends every successor to a history with
$V_{j-1}\geq0$, where the selected lower-layer policy is safe.  Conversely, if $\mu$ preserves
every viable history, then replacing its continuation by the full-history optimum gives
$Q_j(\eta,\mu_j(R_j(\eta)))\geq J_j^\mu(\eta)\geq0$ at every viable history, so every fiber has a
common-safe action and every obstruction vanishes.
\end{proof}
This equivalence is global over all controlled layers.  For a particular initial fiber, a positive
obstruction in an unreachable fiber is irrelevant; the following exhaustive object is local.

Let $\Pi_{1:s}^R=\prod_{j=1}^s\mathsf A^{R_j(\mathsf H_j)}$ be the finite class of all such code
policies and define
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_s^R(z)=\max_{\mu\in\Pi_{1:s}^R}
 \min_{\eta\in F_{s,z}}J_s^\mu(\eta).
 \label{eq:exact-latent-viability}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Exact finite representation-policy viability]
\label{prop:exact-representation-viability}
$\Lambda_s^R(z)\geq0$ if and only if one deterministic time-indexed representation policy keeps
the margin nonnegative on every branch from every history in $F_{s,z}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $\mu$, induction on the recursion for $J_s^\mu$ gives branchwise safety exactly when
$J_s^\mu(\eta)\geq0$.  The fiber minimum imposes this simultaneously on all histories; the finite
policy maximum gives the existence equivalence and attainment.
\end{proof}

The policy enumeration above has an equivalent Bellman recursion that keeps all continuation
decisions coupled.  For a nonempty $S\subseteq\mathsf H_s$, let
\[
 g_s(S)=\min_{\eta\in S}G_s(\eta),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Post}_s^R(S,u)
 =\bigcup_{\eta\in S}N_s\!\left(\eta,u(R_s(\eta))\right),
\]
where $u:R_s(S)\to\mathsf A$ assigns one action to each code currently represented in $S$.
Because $\mathsf A$ is nonempty, $u$ extends arbitrarily to all codes in $R_s(\mathsf H_s)$; those
outside $R_s(S)$ do not affect the current reachable-set value.
Define
\begin{align}
 W_0^R(S)&=g_0(S),\nonumber\\
 W_s^R(S)&=\max_{u\in\mathsf A^{R_s(S)}}
 \min\left\{g_s(S),
 W_{s-1}^R\!\left(\operatorname{Post}_s^R(S,u)\right)\right\}.
 \label{eq:set-representation-bellman}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Exact set-valued representation Bellman recursion]
\label{thm:set-representation-bellman}
For every nonempty $S\subseteq\mathsf H_s$,
\[
 W_s^R(S)=\max_{\mu\in\Pi_{1:s}^R}\min_{\eta\in S}J_s^\mu(\eta).
\]
Thus $W_s^R(S)\geq0$ if and only if one deterministic time-indexed representation policy keeps the
margin nonnegative on every branch from every history in $S$.  Every maximum is attained, and
$\Lambda_s^R(z)=W_s^R(F_{s,z})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The base case is immediate.  At a controlled layer, restrict a policy's current assignment to
$R_s(S)$ and call it $u$; conversely, the preceding arbitrary extension makes every such $u$
admissible.  Write the remaining lower-layer policy as $\nu$.  Associativity of finite minima gives
\[
 \min_{\eta\in S}J_s^{(u,\nu)}(\eta)
 =\min\left\{g_s(S),
 \min_{\eta'\in\operatorname{Post}_s^R(S,u)}J_{s-1}^\nu(\eta')\right\}.
\]
For fixed $u$, finiteness and monotonicity of $x\mapsto\min\{g_s(S),x\}$ permit maximization over
$\nu$ inside that scalar map.  The induction hypothesis supplies $W_{s-1}^R$; maximizing over $u$
gives \eqref{eq:set-representation-bellman}.  The sign equivalence follows from the recursive
definition of $J_s^\mu$, and finiteness gives attainment.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact dynamic viability data processing]
\label{prop:dynamic-viability-data-processing}
Suppose $R_j^c=T_j\circ R_j^f$ on the same history game for every controlled layer
$1\leq j\leq s$.  Then, for every nonempty $S\subseteq\mathsf H_s$,
\[
 W_s^{R^f}(S)\geq W_s^{R^c}(S).
\]
No postprocessing condition is needed at terminal layer zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Lift any coarse policy by $\mu_j^f(z)=\mu_j^c(T_j(z))$.  The lifted policy chooses the same physical
action at every history and therefore has the same recursively evaluated $J$ value.  The fine
policy class contains every lifted coarse behavior, so its maximum cannot be smaller.
\end{proof}

Let $U_s=\{\eta:V_s(\eta)\geq0\}$ and set $\chi_s(R)=0$ when $U_s$ is empty and
$\chi_s(R)=[-W_s^R(U_s)]_+$ otherwise.  The preceding proposition gives
$\chi_s(R^f)\leq\chi_s(R^c)$.  Together with
Proposition~\ref{prop:global-sign-preservation}, Theorem~\ref{thm:set-representation-bellman} gives
the exact three-way sign equivalence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max_{1\leq s\leq T}\chi_s(R)=0
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \kappa_{1:T}^{\max}(R)=0\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists\mu\ \forall s,\eta:\
 V_s(\eta)\geq0\Rightarrow J_s^\mu(\eta)\geq0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, a policy safe on all of $U_s$ supplies at its first layer a common action with
$Q_s\geq J_s^\mu\geq0$ on every viable fiber member, so every $\kappa_s$ vanishes; the converse is
Proposition~\ref{prop:global-sign-preservation}.  The safely retainable family
\[
 \mathfrak V_s(R)=
 \{S\subseteq U_s:S=\varnothing\ \text{or}\ W_s^R(S)\geq0\}
\]
is downward closed, and deterministic coarsening gives
$\mathfrak V_s(R^c)\subseteq\mathfrak V_s(R^f)$.  Its maximum cardinality is exactly the largest
number of full-history viable histories retained by one representation policy.

The one-step obstruction $\kappa_s$ does not compose.  If a two-step singleton root leads to two
same-code one-step histories with Q rows $(1,-1)$ and $(-1,1)$, then $\kappa_2=0$ under
full-history continuation but $\Lambda_2^R=-1$.  The finite oracle therefore enumerates code
policies and also reports the maximum fraction of full-history viable histories retained by one
policy.  Equivalently, the new implementation memoizes
\eqref{eq:set-representation-bellman} and can enumerate $\mathfrak V_s(R)$ under an explicit
exponential guard; randomized small games are checked against independent full-policy enumeration.

Two hypotheses cannot be weakened silently.  Postprocessing only at the initial layer does not
order $W_s$: identical root codes may lead to histories separated by the fine future code and
merged by the coarse future code.  Also, separate nonnegative values $\Lambda_s^R(z)$ need not
combine into one global policy: different initial fibers can require incompatible continuation
actions after reaching the same lower-layer code.  The common-set value $W_s^R(U_s)$ detects that
coupling.

This is a theorem about representation-measurable history policies: $R_s$ may be recomputed from
the full history.  It does not establish a recursive code update, a Markov latent transition, or
planning wholly in latent space; those require additional approximate-information-state premises.

\subsection{Connection to latent robust-Q approximation}

Define the best uniform latent approximation error
\begin{equation}
 \delta_s^*(R_s)=\min_{q:\mathcal Z_s\times\mathsf A\to\mathbb R}
 \max_{\eta,a}|Q_s(\eta,a)-q(R_s(\eta),a)|.
 \label{eq:latent-q-error}
\end{equation}
Midpoint approximation on each finite fiber-action pair gives
\[
 \delta_s^*(R_s)=\frac12\max_{z,a}
 \left(\max_{\eta\in F_{s,z}}Q_s(\eta,a)
       -\min_{\eta\in F_{s,z}}Q_s(\eta,a)\right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Uniform Q approximation]\label{thm:uniform-q-sufficiency}
For every stage, $\rho_s(R_s)\leq2\delta_s^*(R_s)$.  If a particular $q_s$ has uniform error
$\delta_s$ and the code policy is greedy with respect to $q_s$, then its finite-horizon robust
safety-value loss is at most $2\sum_{j=1}^s\delta_j$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a fiber and let $a_q\in\arg\max_a q_s(z,a)$.  Uniform error gives, for every fiber member,
\[
 V_s(\eta)=\max_a Q_s(\eta,a)
 \leq q_s(z,a_q)+\delta_s
 \leq Q_s(\eta,a_q)+2\delta_s.
\]
Thus the selected-action optimal-margin regret of $a_q$ is at most $2\delta_s$.  Minimizing over
actions gives the first claim.  For the particular q-greedy policy, the arbitrary-policy
Theorem~\ref{thm:dynamic-representation-loss} applies with
$\epsilon_s^\mu\leq2\delta_s$ at every stage.
\end{proof}

The factor two is attained by two histories sharing a code and the Q table
$\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$: here
$\rho_1=2$ and $\delta_1^*=1$.  Static current safety is identical for both histories.  The horizon
sum is also tight: an enumerated two-stage tree in the accompanying test suite has
$\rho_1=\rho_2=1$, full-history value two, and minimax margin-policy value zero initially.
Conversely, uniform Q preservation is not necessary: if the same action is optimal throughout a
fiber, $\rho_s$ can vanish while suboptimal action values vary.

\paragraph{Model-local sufficient condition.}
If $V_{s-1}$ is $L_{s-1}$-Lipschitz under a declared history metric, same-code current margins
differ by at most $\alpha_s$, and same-code successor sets have symmetric Hausdorff distance at
most $\beta_s$ for each action, then
\[
 |Q_s(\eta,a)-Q_s(\widetilde\eta,a)|
 \leq\max\{\alpha_s,L_{s-1}\beta_s\},
 \qquad
 \rho_s(R_s)\leq\max\{\alpha_s,L_{s-1}\beta_s\}.
\]
The first inequality follows from the Lipschitz bound on infima over Hausdorff-close sets and the
one-Lipschitzness of the scalar minimum; the second follows from midpoint approximation and
Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-q-sufficiency}.  A raw neural latent radius is not such a bound:
the metric scale, Lipschitz constant, and population successor coverage must be justified.

\paragraph{Conditional Euclidean cover-bound calculation.}
Suppose an audited history set $S=\{\eta_i\}$ is an $r_{\rm cov}$-net under a declared history
metric, the latent metric is explicitly $d_{\mathcal Z}(z,z')=\lVert z-z'\rVert_2$, $R_s$ is
$L_R$-Lipschitz into that norm, every $Q_s(\cdot,a)$ is $L_Q$-Lipschitz, and sampled estimates
have uniform error at most $e_Q$.  Define
\[
 \widehat\Omega_s(r)=
 \max_{\substack{i,j,a:\ d_{\mathcal Z}(R_s(\eta_i),R_s(\eta_j))\leq r}}
 |\widehat Q_s(\eta_i,a)-\widehat Q_s(\eta_j,a)|.
\]
For every population pair with latent distance at most $\delta$, choosing its nearest cover points
and applying the triangle inequality gives
\begin{equation}
 |Q_s(\eta,a)-Q_s(\widetilde\eta,a)|
 \leq\widehat\Omega_s(\delta+2L_Rr_{\rm cov})
 +2L_Qr_{\rm cov}+2e_Q.
 \label{eq:cover-q-certificate}
\end{equation}
At $\delta=0$, the right-hand side upper-bounds $\rho_s(R_s)$ and can be substituted into the
minimax specialization of Theorem~\ref{thm:dynamic-representation-loss}.  The public implementation
is named a conditional Euclidean bound calculator.  It records metric, premise provenance, status,
and an explicit radius-comparison tolerance $\tau_{\rm rad}$; the finite search radius is
$\delta+2L_Rr_{\rm cov}+\tau_{\rm rad}$ and defaults to $\tau_{\rm rad}=0$.  The routine checks
arithmetic, not the cover, Lipschitz, or uniform-error premises, and is not a verified finite-data
certificate or an i.i.d. generalization result.

A separate implementation closes these premises when the declared domain itself is finite and
completely enumerated: it validates the full history-distance matrix, derives the exact cover
radius and pairwise Lipschitz constants, computes sampled-Q error against the full Q table, and
checks the resulting Q-coordinate oscillation bound on every enumerated pair within the operational
latent radius.  This gives a verified bound only on that enumerated domain.  It supplies no coverage
statement for omitted histories, a continuous system, or an external population, and therefore
does not remove the population-certification gate.

\paragraph{Limits and novelty boundary.}
The finite assumptions supply attained, measurable selectors and make the zero endpoint valid.
For action space $(0,1)$ and $Q(a)=a$, the infimum regret is zero but no exact selector attains it;
general spaces require compactness/semicontinuity and measurable selection, or explicit
$\epsilon$-selector slack.  Strong/pathwise randomization does not improve the max--min value;
expected-loss safety is a different theorem.  Sampled histories give lower witnesses, not
population upper bounds, unless coverage assumptions are proved.  Approximate-bisimulation safety
synthesis and belief-support shielding already cover sign-specific abstraction structure
\citep{girard2012safety,sheng2024safe}.  Finally, partial-observation
barrier-value and conformal safety results already use a minimum-over-time margin payoff and
continue to narrow the novelty window \citep{jahanshahi2026cbvf}.  This package should remain an
audit formalism unless paired with a genuinely verified population bound, a sign-specific theorem
not subsumed by symbolic control, or a learned method with a strong matched safety--utility result.
